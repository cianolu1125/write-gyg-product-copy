# GYG copy rules and conversion patterns

## Evidence status

This guide combines three layers:

1. User house rules from `GYG产品描述模板.docx` and later confirmations.
2. Official GetYourGuide supplier guidance reviewed on 2026-08-11.
3. Observed patterns from public GYG listings reviewed on 2026-08-11.

Marketplace patterns are correlations from visible listings, not proof that wording caused their conversion performance. GYG states that ranking also depends on traveler preferences, market behavior, revenue, conversion, availability, reviews, pricing, and other signals.

## Research sample

- Reviewed current destination ranking pages for Paris, Rome, Tokyo, and Dubai, covering more than 30 prominently ranked products.
- Read field-level content from 14 products spanning cruises, attraction tickets, guided heritage tours, day trips, food tours, cooking classes, cultural shows, desert safaris, water sports, and yacht tours.
- Prioritized products labeled Top pick, Top rated, Likely to sell out, or showing large review volumes.
- Do not copy their wording. Use only generalized structural patterns.

## Official source rules

### Product title

Use `Location/City: Activity Type + Inclusion or USP`. For attraction tickets, use `Attraction: Entry Ticket + Inclusion or USP`. Use title case. Make the activity immediately clear. Do not include price, unsupported trademarks, all-caps words, spelling errors, or subjective claims such as “best” or “amazing.”

House range: 57-60 visible characters including spaces and punctuation. This is an explicit user-confirmed house range, not an official GYG platform limit.

Build the title only after ranking the Highlights. Integrate the highest-priority attraction keywords and strongest verified service differentiator wherever they fit clearly. The title is the primary click driver; do not settle for a generic activity label while stronger verified selling points remain unused.

### Short description

Write 2-3 sentences that appear with the title and image in search. Lead with the most interesting, concrete experience. Do not paste sentences from the full description. Do not use unlicensed trademarks.

House range: 190-200 visible characters including spaces and punctuation. Write 2-3 sentences. This is an explicit user-confirmed house range, not an official GYG platform limit.

### Full description

Explain exactly what customers will see and do. Use original, descriptive language. GYG advises against merely listing a schedule. When a day tour uses chronological nodes, treat time/place headings only as navigation; make the paragraphs vivid, customer-centered, and outcome-led. Tickets and experiences instead use selling-point headings as defined below.

House length: 2,000-2,300 visible characters including spaces and punctuation. Every substantive stop body must be 200-400 characters. Headings and departure-detail text count toward the total; newline controls do not. Combine low-value transfers and do not pad with generic praise.

Choose structure by product type. A `day_tour` uses chronological itinerary nodes. A `ticket` or `experience` uses non-time-based selling-point sections, still introduced with separate `▼` headings and 200-400-character bodies. Never invent a timeline for a product whose value is access, participation, content, flexibility, or atmosphere rather than a sequence of stops.

### Highlights

Use exactly 5 under the house rule. Start each with a verb. Keep each informative, unique, and non-repetitive. Every highlight must be 75-80 visible characters including spaces and punctuation.

### Inclusions and exclusions

Use plain item labels, not promotional adjectives. State hotel pickup and drop-off precisely. Optional items that materially change the offer should be configured as options rather than buried as exclusions when the platform setup requires it.

### Options

Create options only for material variations such as duration, group type, meeting point, pickup area/price, language-linked configuration, inclusions, or route. Keep option titles parallel and easy to compare. Use a description only if the title and inclusion fields cannot explain the difference. Do not add a sales sentence merely to fill space.

Under the user's house rule, every supplied option requires a Description of 180-200 characters. Put the exact content and difference first, then use the remaining space for a truthful high-conversion payoff. Keep titles as short as possible while making the distinguishing attribute immediately visible.

### Meeting point and pickup

Use short, simple directions. State what the customer should look for, when to arrive, and the exact pickup/meeting arrangement. Pickup and meeting-point versions require separate options; distinct meeting points generally require separate options. State the real activity start time in Availability, not the start of a pickup window.

### Know before you go and voucher

Use strict separation. Put participation and execution details only in Voucher Information: exact meeting/pickup instructions, arrival lead time, recognition cues, contact timing, phone/check-in instructions, and what to do if a confirmation message is missing. Put disclaimers and cautions only in Know Before You Go: eligibility, accessibility, luggage, weather dependence, route variability, optional costs, driver/guide limitations, late/no-show, cancellation, and refund conditions. A standing voucher sentence may say that pickup or vehicle details will be sent by email or WhatsApp the day before, but never insert a specific address or number.

## Conversion patterns observed by field

### Titles: compress the buying decision

Prominent listings usually combine a searchable anchor with one or two concrete differentiators: attraction plus access type, destination set plus transport, activity plus tangible inclusion, or route plus group/pickup feature. Numbers work when they express real scope, such as a count of tastings, dishes, sites, levels, or duration. Avoid vague emotion words when a concrete inclusion can do more work.

Prioritize in this order:

1. Exact location or origin
2. Recognizable activity or landmark
3. Highest-value verified differentiator
4. Secondary inclusion only if space remains

### Short descriptions: show the whole purchase in one breath

Strong visible snippets commonly use this sequence:

1. Active verb plus core experience
2. Two or three concrete proof points: landmarks, count, access, meal, transport, expert, or participation
3. Convenience or emotional payoff

Prefer specific nouns and verbs over adjective stacks. Make the first sentence understandable without the title. Do not promise an outcome controlled by weather, crowds, wildlife, or third parties.

For route products, prioritize the strongest whole-tour value: efficient coverage, complementary scenery, iconic and distinctive stops, photo variety, small-group comfort, or pickup convenience. Avoid comma-heavy landmark inventories. Use two flowing sentences when that reads more naturally.

Use an inviting introduction tone. Short Description is sales copy, so keep operational instructions, disclaimers, cautions, and policy language in their assigned fields.

### Highlights: distribute five distinct reasons to book

Assign one job to each line:

1. Signature experience or must-see anchor
2. Second major attraction, route, or breadth of experience
3. Participation, learning, sensory, or photo payoff
4. Differentiating service: access, expert, small group, transport, pickup
5. Tangible inclusion, comfort, flexibility, or memorable takeaway

Use verbs such as `Explore`, `Visit`, `Discover`, `Enjoy`, `Taste`, `Learn`, `Cruise`, `Capture`, `Ride`, or `Travel`. Avoid five synonyms describing the same view.

At least two highlights should normally explain why the overall product is easier, richer, more comfortable, or more efficient—not merely name attractions. For a multi-stop route, cover the route's breadth, experiential variety, photo or participation value, service format, and convenience.

### Full description: convert through clarity and progression

High-performing examples tend to progress clearly through the customer journey. In this house template, the departure node—not an extra introductory paragraph—opens the experience. Each body should contain at least two of these:

- customer action
- concrete sight, taste, skill, or interaction
- verified inclusion or access benefit
- expert or operational support
- sensory detail grounded in fact
- why the stop matters
- transition that maintains momentum

Use second person. Keep the customer as the grammatical subject more often than the operator. Replace “This tour offers...” with what the customer does or receives. Mention optional items and limitations where they become relevant, not after creating a false expectation.

For itinerary products, use this pattern:

`▼ time - place/activity (duration)`

`Body: arrive/action -> specific experience -> differentiating payoff -> truthful transition or caveat`

Put the heading on its own line, optional pickup/meeting details as real bullets under the departure heading, and the 200-400-character body immediately afterward. Leave a blank line between nodes. Do not add separate `opening` or `closing` paragraphs; use the departure and return nodes. Do not turn every transfer into a promotional paragraph.

Write every node from scratch. Vary the opening words and sentence rhythm across stops. Do not repeatedly begin with `Discover`, `Experience`, `At`, or `Next`. Avoid generic emotional filler such as `unforgettable` when a concrete scene or customer action can carry the appeal.

Every body is persuasive introduction copy: help the traveler picture the stop and understand its value. Do not interrupt the journey with weather, refund, meeting, luggage, or late-arrival warnings.

### Options: reduce comparison friction

Parallel option titles convert better operationally because customers can scan the single changed attribute. Examples of structures:

- `Tokyo Station Meeting Point` / `Shinjuku Meeting Point`
- `Shared Tour with Pickup` / `Shared Tour without Pickup`
- `Standard Seating` / `Front-Row Seating`
- `Morning Departure` / `Sunset Departure`

Do not claim that an option is “premium” unless the inclusions clearly substantiate the term.

When the user confirms options, every option must have a title and description. State the material difference first, then any minimum booking size or restriction, followed by option-specific meeting/pickup and availability details. Make parallel options easy to compare.

### Meeting and pickup: sell confidence, not excitement

This block should remove anxiety. Include the exact place or pickup area, arrival lead time, recognition cue, contact timing, and what happens if the customer is late. Avoid scenic copy and avoid repeating the itinerary.

### Important information: protect conversion by preventing surprise

Transparent constraints support customer confidence and reviews. Use one fact per bullet. Put the most booking-relevant restriction first. Phrase caveats calmly and specifically; never hide a material limitation behind marketing language.

## Editing checklist

- Make every claim traceable to source facts.
- Use consistent names, times, duration, group cap, languages, and inclusions across all fields.
- Distinguish visit, entry, pass-by, photo stop, free time, and optional extra.
- Remove generic claims that could describe any competitor.
- Remove repeated landmark lists from adjacent fields.
- Make the five highlights sell the whole tour rather than functioning as five stop labels.
- Check title number placement so `6 Stops Small Group Tour` cannot be mistaken for a six-person group; separate the stop count from the group claim or state a verified cap explicitly.
- Use original wording and distinct paragraph openings; never imitate competitor or source prose.
- Correct spelling, grammar, spacing, title case, and punctuation.
- Avoid `best`, `amazing`, `unforgettable`, `must-do`, `guaranteed`, and similar unsupported claims.
- Avoid urgency or scarcity claims unless supplied by GYG dynamically; never write “likely to sell out” into supplier copy.
- Do not promise views of Mt. Fuji, sunsets, wildlife, traffic-free travel, or skip-the-line access without exact support.
- Keep email and WhatsApp generic unless task-specific contact details are explicitly supplied.
- Validate all hard limits from `field-rules.json`; its optional-section ranges are recommendations only and must never cause padding.
