# Destination, competitor, and selling-point research

Use this workflow whenever the user supplies only a rough itinerary or when current destination/competitor context could improve positioning.

## 1. Build a destination knowledge base

Create a task-local folder such as `gyg-research/<product-slug>/` and save `destination-knowledge.md` with:

- place name, official English name, aliases, and location;
- what travelers actually see or do;
- cultural, historical, natural, visual, or sensory significance;
- visit type: entry, exterior view, photo stop, free time, pass-by, activity, or transfer;
- realistic time needs, physical demands, seasonal variation, visibility/access limitations, and common traveler confusion;
- stable facts that can support copy;
- dated source links and a confidence label for each fact.

Prefer official attraction sites, tourism boards, transport operators, local government, and other primary sources. Use reputable secondary sources only to fill gaps. Never use an unsourced travel-blog claim as a product promise.

## 2. Scan same-place competitors

Create `competitor-scan.md`. Search current GYG listings that visit the same places, plus the closest substitutes departing from the same origin.

- Target 10-50 relevant products when the market supplies enough visible listings.
- If fewer than 10 are discoverable, analyze all usable results and state the coverage limitation.
- Record search date, URL, title, route, duration, group format, pickup/meeting model, notable inclusions, visible rating/review or popularity signals, headline selling points, and customer-expectation risks.
- Separate direct competitors from partial-route and substitute products.
- Treat price, rank, availability, reviews, and popularity labels as volatile snapshots.
- Do not copy phrases. Extract patterns and gaps only.

Analyze by field:

- Title: keyword order, scope, differentiator, ambiguity.
- Short description: first hook, proof points, convenience payoff.
- Highlights: five distinct purchase reasons versus repeated place names.
- Long description: how stops are made meaningful, expectation setting, route flow.
- Options: how choices and restrictions are made easy to compare.
- Operational content: pickup confidence, weather, mobility, luggage, child rules, late/no-show clarity.

## 3. Produce the selling-point brief

Create `selling-point-brief.md` before drafting. Include:

1. The five strongest verified reasons to book. Rank visible attractions first by popularity, traveler recognition, visual appeal, and purchase influence; then rank verified soft-service differentiators.
2. Evidence for each reason from itinerary facts and destination context.
3. Competitor parity points that are necessary but not differentiating.
4. Genuine differentiators and underserved traveler needs.
5. Claims that must not be made because they are unverified, weather-dependent, or competitor-only.
6. A field map showing which selling point belongs in title, short description, each highlight, each long-description node, and option copy.

The five selling points must describe the whole product, not merely list five attractions. Useful lenses include:

- breadth achieved in limited time;
- complementary scenery or experience types;
- iconic plus less-obvious stops;
- photo variety and memorable visual compositions;
- small-group comfort or verified group cap;
- hotel pickup or simpler logistics;
- less planning, fewer transfers, or better route coherence;
- transparent preparation that reduces surprises.

Create a title compression shortlist containing the top attraction keywords and strongest service differentiators. Test combinations until the title reaches 57-60 characters without ambiguity or keyword stuffing.

## 4. Reuse and refresh

- Reuse stable, sourced destination knowledge when the same place appears again.
- Recheck official access, closures, schedules, seasonal conditions, and all competitor snapshots for each new product task.
- Add a `last_verified` date to reused facts.
- If internet access is unavailable, say so, use only supplied/previously verified facts, and do not present the competitor scan as current.
