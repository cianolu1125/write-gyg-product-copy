# Traveler-view completeness and clarification gate

Review the product as a first-time visitor deciding whether to book and how to participate without surprises.

## Critical questions before final drafting

Ask the user when the answer is missing, conflicting, or materially affects expectations:

- What exactly is included at each stop: admission, guided entry, exterior visit, photo stop, free time, meal, tasting, or optional purchase?
- What is the total duration, actual departure time, expected return, and realistic traffic/season variability?
- Is the product shared, small-group, or private? What is the verified maximum group size?
- Is the driver also the guide? What commentary and languages are actually provided?
- Is pickup included, optional, geographically limited, or restricted by minimum booking size?
- Where is the meeting point, how early should guests arrive, and how will they recognize the vehicle/host?
- What do infants and children pay? Do they occupy seats? How far in advance is a child seat request required?
- What luggage, mobility, stair, clothing, age, identification, or accessibility constraints apply?
- What weather-dependent outcomes cannot be guaranteed, and what schedule/route changes are possible?
- Which cancellation, rescheduling, late-arrival, no-show, or operator-cancellation rules are authoritative?
- What exactly differs between every option?

Ask only the smallest prioritized question set needed to remove critical ambiguity. Explain briefly why each answer affects the guest. Do not ask the user for destination marketing facts that can be researched independently.

## Policy-writing rules

- Preserve the restriction; improve how its reason is explained.
- Use friendly, direct language instead of legalistic or evasive wording.
- Never soften a non-refundable or eligibility rule until its meaning becomes ambiguous.
- Do not invent a refund rule from weather risk. Confirm the authoritative policy first.
- Use `between 07:30 and 08:00` for a window and consistent 24-hour times.
- Say that meeting time may be slightly adjusted only when operationally true.
- Generic standing wording may state that final pickup, driver, or vehicle details will be shared by email or WhatsApp one day before departure.

## Option completeness

When options exist, build them as parallel purchase choices. Every option needs:

- a concise value-led title;
- a description that states the material difference first;
- its option-specific eligibility, minimum booking size, or caution;
- option-specific inclusions/exclusions where applicable;
- meeting/pickup instructions or an explicit `Not applicable` state;
- the actual activity start/departure time, not the pickup-window start.

Lead with value, then state the restriction clearly. Example structure: `Small Group Tour with Hotel Pickup (Min. 2 Guests)`. Avoid vague labels such as `Standard`, `Premium`, or `Option 1` without a concrete difference.

## Final traveler test

Before delivery, confirm a visitor can answer:

1. What will I do and why is this route worth choosing?
2. What is included, excluded, optional, or only viewed from outside?
3. Where and when do I start and finish?
4. Which option should I choose and what changes?
5. What could change because of weather, traffic, or operations?
6. What must I prepare, pay, request, or understand before booking?
